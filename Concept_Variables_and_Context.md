# Variables and Context

### Context
All MLB executives know that every MLB park can be vastly different from one another and can vastly change how the game is managed and front office decisions are made. It is one of the few sports that doesn't have strict limitations on the dimensions of the field. This can vastly change the identity of a team or player. The front office of the Phillies or Rays, whose stadiums sit at 20 and 10 feet above sea level, might try to sign better pitchers, or contact and speed players.
However the  the Diamondbacks or Braves, who play at an elevation of 1090 and 1050 feet, may sign more power hitters and only have a couple defensive players to balance out the team. If General Managers can find exactly what makes a player thrive at a specific type of stadium, they can sign more of those players, and theoretically win a dozen or so games and hopefully qualify for the postseason.

### Variables in Use
There are only a few stats that need to be tracked across all 30 teams, and most of them can never be applied to two teams and carry the same weight. The best power based statistics are:
- Home Runs
- On Base %
- Slugging %
- OPS
- OPS+
- bWAR
- Strikeout rate
- HR/9 or AB/HR
