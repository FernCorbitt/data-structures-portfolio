# Project 2 Context and Research

Quantifying player skill is a huge part of baseball in every aspect of the game. Owners want to avoid overpaying players or putting too much money into a failing franchise. Especially if they have been a historically bad team like the Angels or Rockies.
Similarly, front-office staff work within a relative budget, often set by the team Owner and/or President. It’s always a huge risk to sign any player to a large multi-year deal, especially in recent years as most teams shift to a power-focused approach, resulting in lower offensive output across the league. 
Having a single value retaining to certain situations could be an important factor for Managers as well. Picking the right player off the bench or bringing in the right bullpen is often the biggest factor in a game's result. With growing datasets and more accurate selections, there is less risk in every substitution, which should theoretically lead to a team's success.s
Like many, if not all, baseball fans, I am curious about the winter Collective Bargaining Agreement (CBA) meetings in January and the effects it could have on the future. The majority opinion is that the owners and players won’t compromise on many different levels, the most controversial being the idea of implementing a salary cap. This will lead to a shortened season like the strike in the mid-90s or even canceling the season entirely.
